Understanding copyright

Know what copyright means and how it affects the content you create, share and reuse.

What is copyright?

Copyright is a form of legal protection for original creative works. It generally gives the copyright owner control over certain uses of their work, subject to applicable law and exceptions.

What kinds of work can be protected?

  • Photographs, illustrations and graphic designs.
  • Music, sound recordings and compositions.
  • Films, videos and other audiovisual works.
  • Books, articles, scripts and other written works.
  • Software and other eligible creative works.

Examples for content creators

  • Using a photographer's image in a business advert without permission may raise copyright concerns.
  • Adding a popular song to a monetised video may require the relevant rights or licence.
  • Reposting someone's design does not automatically make it free to use.

How to respect copyright

  1. Create your own original material where possible.
  2. Ask the rights holder for permission when needed.
  3. Read the licence terms before using stock or Creative Commons material.
  4. Follow any conditions, such as attribution or limits on commercial use.
  5. Keep records of permissions, licences and sources.
Remember: Giving credit is good practice, but credit alone does not replace permission where permission is required. Copyright rules and exceptions can depend on the circumstances and Botswana law.

For legal advice about a specific situation, consult a qualified legal professional or the relevant Botswana authority.
