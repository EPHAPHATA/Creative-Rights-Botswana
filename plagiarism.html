Understanding plagiarism

Learn what plagiarism looks like, why it matters and how to avoid it in creative and digital work.

What is plagiarism?

Plagiarism is presenting another person's words, ideas or creative work as your own without proper acknowledgement. It can be intentional or accidental.

Different forms of plagiarism

  • Direct plagiarism: copying words or content exactly without quotation or acknowledgement.
  • Self-plagiarism: reusing your own previously submitted or published work where disclosure or permission is expected.
  • Mosaic plagiarism: combining copied phrases or ideas from sources without proper acknowledgement.
  • Paraphrasing plagiarism: changing a few words while keeping another person's idea or structure without credit.
  • Media plagiarism: using another creator's photo, video, design or music and presenting it as your own.

Examples

  • Copying a caption from another creator and posting it under your name.
  • Downloading a photograph and removing the watermark before using it in an advert.
  • Rewriting an article by changing a few words without acknowledging the original source.
  • Using another person's video footage in your own video without permission or acknowledgement.

How to avoid plagiarism

  1. Make your own ideas and original content the starting point.
  2. Credit the source when you use another person's ideas or words.
  3. Use quotation marks for exact words where appropriate.
  4. Paraphrase genuinely and still acknowledge the original source.
  5. Check the licence and obtain permission where required before using media.
  6. Keep a record of links, authors, dates and permissions.
Copyright and plagiarism are not the same. Copyright concerns legal rights over protected works. Plagiarism concerns presenting another person's work or ideas without proper acknowledgement. One action can involve both, but not every case of plagiarism is automatically copyright infringement.
